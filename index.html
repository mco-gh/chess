      About Chess

      Chess for two people, wherever they are. There are no accounts, no ads and no saved games: just a board and a friend. No friend handy? Play the computer.

      Starting a game

      1. Pick white, black or a surprise, add a clock or learning mode if you like, then choose Start a game.
      2. Send the code (or the link) to your friend.
      3. When they join, white moves first.

      Playing

      • Tap a piece to see where it can go, then tap a marked square. Or just drag it there.
      • Only legal moves are offered, including castling (move the king two squares) and capturing en passant.
      • A pawn that reaches the far side becomes the piece you choose.
      • The game ends by checkmate or resignation, or in a draw by stalemate, agreement, the same position three times, fifty moves with no capture or pawn move, or too few pieces left to mate.
      • With a clock, each of you gets the chosen time for the whole game, plus any added time after every move. The clocks start once white has moved. Run out and you lose, unless your opponent has only a king left, which is a draw.
      • Play again starts a rematch with the colors swapped and the same clock.
      • Analyze this game opens the finished game in Analyze, which steps through it with Stockfish: who was winning, the best moves, and every blunder and great move.

      Learning mode

      Whoever starts a game decides whether it’s a learning game, with the Learning mode switch above Start a game. It’s the same for both players. In a learning game you see an evaluation above the board: a bar showing how the game stands and a score in pawns (+1.0 is roughly a pawn ahead for white; “mate in 3” means a forced checkmate). It comes from Stockfish, thinking in your browser for a couple of seconds on each position.

      You also get Take back, which undoes your last move (and the reply to it, if there was one). Against the computer it happens straight away. Against a friend, each take-back needs their OK.

      Playing the computer

      Pick a strength and choose Play the computer. Strengths are Elo ratings, the scale chess uses: about 1350 for a club newcomer, 2200 for a master, 2500 for a grandmaster. Beginner and Novice are gentler still. Max is the engine at full strength, far beyond any human.

      The computer is Stockfish, the free, open-source chess engine, running right in your browser (so it works offline too). Its ratings are its own estimates, calibrated against other engines, so treat them as a guide. There’s no clock against the computer, and it doesn’t take draw offers.

      Video and chat

      Turn on your camera or microphone at any time during a game, or just type. Each of you decides separately what to share.

      Privacy

      Chess is a static page. A public PeerJS server introduces your two browsers using the game code; after that, moves, chat, video and audio travel directly between you over WebRTC. Nothing is recorded, and only your color settings are saved, in this browser.

      Resign this game?

      Your opponent wins. You can play again afterwards.

      Leave this game?

      The game ends for both of you and can’t be resumed.

      Connection lost

      The link to your opponent dropped.
